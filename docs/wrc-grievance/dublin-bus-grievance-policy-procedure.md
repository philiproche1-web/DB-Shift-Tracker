## Page 14–15

If a satisfactory outcome is not reached, the supervisor or employee can
refer the grievance to their manager in writing. If the grievance relates
to the behaviour of the supervisor or if the employee feels that it is
not possible to approach the supervisor, the grievance may be brought to
formal stage of the procedure.

### Stage 1: Formal procedure

The formal procedure can be used if you feel that:

- the informal approach has not worked
- mediation, if used, has not worked, or
- the informal procedure is not appropriate or something very serious
  has happened

You should put the complaint in writing, stating you are making a formal
complaint under the grievance procedure, sign and date it, and send it
directly to your immediate supervisor/manager. The complaint must be
received in writing at this stage to invoke the formal grievance
procedure even if the complaint was received in writing at the informal
discussions stage.

...a meeting will be held between the local manager and the employee
raising the matter within a maximum of **14 days**. A summary note of the
meeting will be kept and read out at the end of the meeting. The summary
note of the meeting will be signed by all present. Copies will be issued
as soon as possible thereafter to those in attendance. A determination
will be issued within a further **seven days**.

### Grievance about another person

If your grievance is about another person, your written grievance must
include clear and specific details of actual things that happened with
dates and times as well as the names of anyone who witnessed it.

Your manager will meet with you within a maximum of **14 days**. The
options available to you in relation to the written grievance will be
discussed. A summary note of the meeting will be kept and read out at the
end of the meeting. The summary note of the meeting will be signed by all
present and copies will be issued to those in attendance as soon as
possible after the meeting.

If the complaint involves witnesses/other parties, the time frame for the
investigation may be extended by a further **7 days** to allow the
manager to interview all relevant parties.

A determination will be issued within a further **seven days**.

## Page 16–17

### Agreement and review

If the investigating officer (relevant manager) is unavailable for a
period exceeding **14 days** at any stage during the investigation, the
complaint should be forwarded to the Human Resources Services Manager so
another manager can be appointed to investigate the grievance in their
absence.

This option can only be exercised in circumstances where the relevant
manager is unavailable within the agreed time frame due to annual leave,
illness, etc.

We will give this policy to all Dublin Bus employees and we will include
it in induction and other training programmes for employees.

We will monitor and review this policy regularly to ensure that is it
working and up to date.

The Dublin Bus Grievance Policy and Procedures set out in this booklet
are agreed between the Dublin Bus and the trade unions.

**Human Resources Department — November 2023**

### Stage 2: Appeal

An appeal must be made in writing to the Human Resources Services Manager
within **seven days** of receipt of a local manager's decision clearly
outlining the grounds on which the appeal is being made.

A meeting will then be held within **10 days** and a response/
determination will be issued to the employee within **seven days** of the
meeting date. A signed summary note of the meeting will be kept and
copies will be issued as soon as possible thereafter to those in
attendance.

### External procedures

If you feel that the response following Stage 2 is inadequate you may
refer the grievance to the appropriate external body, e.g. Workplace
Relations Commission.

Steps 4–6 (the "Formal" band of the flowchart) are annotated with this

1. Put grievance in writing to manager

3. An employee representative may accompany the individual raising the
   grievance
4. Witnesses are interviewed
5. Summary notes signed by all parties
6. Response to employee within 7 days
7. Appeal to HR within 7 days
8. Meeting with HR within 10 days
